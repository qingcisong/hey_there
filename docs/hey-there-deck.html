The problem5

Hey There is for teenagers on those days.

You talk through your day. It listens to how you say it, not just what you say. Then it offers one small thing that might help right now.

Who it’s for

High schoolers who wouldn’t call a hotline and wouldn’t book a therapist — but would talk to a friend, if a friend happened to be around.

What it does

Reads tone and pacing against your own voice, reflects it back for you to confirm, and suggests one action that fits your day.

Purpose and audience6

It starts with a phone call.

No form, no mood slider, no 1-to-10. You pick up and talk the way you’d talk to anyone.

If you type, you edit.

When you talk, you hesitate. You pause. You reach for a word and don’t find it. That’s part of what the app reads — and it’s the part a text box throws away.

While you speak, the cloud reacts. Less like typing into a box, more like someone is listening.

Voice gives clues, not conclusions.

When you stop, it says what it noticed. Then you get the final say — agree, correct it, or say there’s more and keep talking. The app never files a verdict you didn’t sign off on.

Then one thing to try.

It looks at the weather, where you are, and what your week has looked like, and picks what fits right now. Today it sees four clear hours ahead, so it suggests a walk.

In Florida, “it’s sunny” has a shelf life of about ten minutes — so it checks the next few hours, not this second. If it were raining, or if you’d been alone all week, it would pick something else.

What it’s built on

Component model for the call screen, the reflection, and the suggestion card.

How it hears you

The live layer — everything that runs during a call

OpenAI Realtime API

Speech in, speech out, low enough latency that it feels like a call rather than a form.

SenseVoice / FunASR

Reads emotion and paralinguistics straight from the audio — not from the transcript.

Open-Meteo

Free hourly forecast, no key required — the next few hours where you are.

The live layer13

Let me show you one piece of it.

That first minute measures your baseline: your speaking rate, how much your pitch moves, how long your pauses run.

Every call after that is compared to you — not to an average person. A slow, flat voice means completely different things depending on whose voice it is.
